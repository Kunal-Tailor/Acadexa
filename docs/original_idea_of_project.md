## 4. Problem statement

A university produces attendance records, marks, assignment and quiz results, and LMS activity, usually in separate systems. That makes it hard to answer questions such as:

- How does attendance relate to marks, and does the relationship differ between schools?
- Does LMS engagement move with performance, or only in some programs?
- Do drops in LMS activity and late submissions show up before marks fall?
- Which courses are consistently weak once course difficulty is accounted for?
- Which students show a change in engagement over a semester?

The system integrates the sources and runs large-scale analysis across them.

## 5. XYZ University model

### 5.1 Hierarchy

```text
University
  └── School
        └── Department
              └── Program (batch, semester)
                    └── Course
                          └── Student (enrolled per semester)
```

Every dashboard view and most Mongo lookups follow this path.

### 5.2 The six schools *(proposed)*

The schools are chosen to behave differently, so cross-school comparisons show real variation.

| School | Students | Behavior baked into the generator |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering & Technology | 3,000 | Assignment and lab heavy, moderate LMS use |
| Computer Engineering & Technology | 2,600 | Very high LMS and quiz activity, coding-style assessments |
| Business | 2,400 | Attendance-sensitive, case and assignment heavy |
| Design | 1,200 | Project-based, few quizzes, lumpy submission timing |
| Health Sciences | 1,500 | High attendance, exam-weighted, steady LMS use |
| Liberal Arts | 1,300 | Reading and forum heavy, fewer quizzes |
| **Total** | **12,000** | |

Rough catalogue *(proposed)*: about 18 departments, 40 programs, 250 courses, around 6 courses per student per semester.

### 5.3 Extending later

Schools, departments, programs and their behavior parameters live in one config file. A 7th school means adding an entry, not changing code.

## 7. The synthetic data generator

Since the data is synthetic, the generator is a core component and needs a written design.

### 7.1 How students are simulated

- Each student gets hidden traits: **ability**, **diligence**, **engagement**, plus a school and program.
- Each course gets a **difficulty** value.
- Attendance, LMS activity, submission timing and scores are drawn from those traits with noise. The school profile (section 5.2) shifts the parameters.
- Events follow a weekly rhythm and a semester rhythm: fewer on weekends, spikes before deadlines and exams, a dip mid-semester.
- Around 8% of students are "decliners" whose engagement fades during the semester, with marks dropping a few weeks later. This is what gives trend detection something real to find.
- Seeded and reproducible, so both teammates generate identical data.

### 7.2 Planted relationships (ground truth)

The generator writes a registry of what it planted:

| Relationship | Planted as |
|---|---|
| Attendance to marks | Moderate positive |
| LMS engagement to marks | Moderate positive, strength varies by school |
| Late submission rate to marks | Negative |
| Course difficulty | A confounder that shifts marks and other variables together |
| Decoy pair (e.g. forum posts vs marks in one school) | **No** planted relationship |

### 7.3 Why this matters

Analytics on planted data would prove nothing on their own. Because we know the truth, the pipeline gets a **recovery test**: do the Spark results find the planted relationships, and do they leave the decoy alone? A results table comparing the two goes into the report.

We claim the system demonstrates the pipeline and analytics at scale. We do not claim to have discovered facts about real students.

### 7.4 Injected data-quality problems (light) *(proposed rates)*

| Problem | Target rate |
|---|---|
| Duplicate LMS events | ~1% |
| Nulls in non-key fields (duration, resource_id, score) | ~1-2% |
| Timestamp format inconsistencies | ~1% |
| `course_id` casing or whitespace problems | ~0.5% |
| Orphan records (student_id not in `students`) | ~0.1% |
| Out-of-range values (negative duration, attendance above 100%) | ~0.1% |

Key fields are never nulled. The generator saves an **injection manifest** with the exact counts, so the cleaning job can be checked against what was injected.

### 7.5 Output mechanics

Generation writes in chunks straight to Parquet (Polars, or NumPy + PyArrow), partitioned by month. Building 70M rows in memory in pandas will fail before Spark starts.

## 8. Analytics modules (tiered) *(proposed)*

| Tier | Modules | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep** | Cross-factor correlation, trend and decline detection, risk indicator | Spark |
| **Medium** | LMS engagement analytics, academic performance analytics | Spark for the big aggregations, Mongo for drill-downs |
| **Thin** | Attendance stats, assignment and quiz stats | Mongo aggregation pipelines |

### 8.1 Cross-factor analysis

- Pairs: Attendance-Performance, LMS engagement-Performance, Assignment lateness-Performance, Quiz attempts-Performance, and attendance + engagement combined.
- Values are **z-scored within course** before correlating, so course difficulty does not distort the result.
- Spearman correlation by default (engagement counts are skewed). Pearson can be shown alongside.
- Reported per school and overall.
- Rule: correlation is reported as observed association, never as cause.

### 8.2 Trend and decline detection

- Weekly engagement per student-course, compared over rolling 4-week windows.
- Score trend across the last graded items of a course.
- Output: a decline flag and the week it started, which is what the risk indicator consumes.

## 9. Rule-based risk indicator

Five signals, each worth one point, thresholds tunable *(proposed)*:

| # | Signal | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low attendance | Term-to-date attendance below 75% |
| 2 | LMS decline | Events in last 4 weeks at or below 50% of the previous 4 weeks |
| 3 | Late submissions | More than 30% of assignments late this semester |
| 4 | Marks decline | Average of last 3 graded items at least 10 points below the student's earlier average in that course |
| 5 | Low quiz activity | Quiz attempts in the bottom quartile of the student's course |

Output looks like `STU010482: 3 / 5 signals (attendance low, LMS declining, late submissions)`.

Wording rule: this is a count of observed signals. It does not predict failure.

## 10. Architecture

```text
                 ┌────────────────────────────┐
                 │  Synthetic data generator   │
                 │ (config, seed, ground truth)│
                 └───────┬─────────────┬───────┘
                         │             │
              raw Parquet│             │core collections + LMS slice
            (~70M events)│             │
                         ▼             ▼
                    ┌─────────┐   ┌──────────┐
                    │  Spark  │   │ MongoDB  │
                    │ (local) │   │ (serving)│
                    └────┬────┘   └────▲─────┘
                         │ gold        │
                         └─────────────┘
                                       │
                                       ▼
                                 Streamlit app
```

### 10.1 Who does what

| Layer | MongoDB | Spark |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Serving and operational store | Batch analytics engine |
| Does | CRUD, indexed lookups, drill-down queries, small aggregation pipelines, time-series slice | Cleaning, dedup, big joins, feature engineering, correlations, trend detection, risk signals |
| Reads | Its own collections | Raw Parquet, and Mongo core collections through the connector |
| Writes | n/a | Gold collections back into Mongo |

The dashboard reads Mongo only. Spark does the heavy work offline and hands over finished results.

| Item | Estimate |

| Spark driver memory | 6-8 GB |
| Mongo cache (set explicitly) | 2-3 GB |

Loading all 70M events into Mongo as documents would take roughly 10+ GB and starve Spark, which is why only a slice goes in. The ~15 events/day figure is an assumption; every number here is good to about plus or minus 30-50% until the generator runs.

## 22. Short explanation for professor / viva

> We are building an Integrated Student Academic Intelligence System for Big Data Technologies. It models a multi-school university with 12,000 students over two academic years, using a seeded synthetic dataset of about 70 million LMS events plus attendance, marks and coursework records. The data is stored in MongoDB for serving and in Parquet for batch processing, and PySpark handles cleaning, joins, correlation and trend analysis. Results are written back to MongoDB and shown in a Streamlit dashboard. Because we generated the data, we can test whether our analytics recover the relationships we planted, and we benchmark Spark against pandas and MongoDB aggregation to show where each tool fits.
